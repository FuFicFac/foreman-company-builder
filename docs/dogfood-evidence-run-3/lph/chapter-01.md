# Chapter 1 — The Quiet Hours

Mara kept the night desk at Harbor Municipal because nobody else wanted the hours
between closing and the first bus. The building ticked. The radiator argued with
itself. Outside, rain stitched the windows into a single gray sheet.

At 1:17 a.m. the intercom clicked twice—not a visitor, not maintenance. A
pattern she had started marking in a paper notebook after the third week: two
clicks, sixty seconds of silence, one longer click. Always from the east stair.

She did not go to the stair. She logged the time, initialed the page, and put
the notebook back under the blotter where the day shift would never look.

By 2:40 the clicks stopped. At 3:05 a work order appeared in the system with
tomorrow's date already stamped, filed under "routine filter swap," signed with
a set of initials that were not hers and not any name on the night roster.

Mara copied the order number into the notebook. Then she locked the drawer,
turned the desk lamp one notch lower, and waited for the building to finish
pretending it was empty.
